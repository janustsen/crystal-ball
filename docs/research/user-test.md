# First five user sessions

Target: adults aged 18–24 who already use an astrology app or share playful quizzes. No representative sample is claimed; no participant sessions have happened. Pricing and sharing willingness in this age group remain untested. Do not recruit or send messages without separate authorization.

Recruit 2 students, 2 early-career workers and 1 person who dislikes astrology. Compensate only after separate budget approval. Avoid collecting birth details, health or financial documents. Use fictional question fixtures if preferred. Record observations with consent; use anonymous participant IDs.

1. “What do you think this app does? What does the ball mean to you?” (30 seconds, no explanation.)
2. “Ask something you'd actually be curious about next year.” Observe tap discovery, typing/voice and how much personal context they volunteer. Stop sensitive questions with the product redirect.
3. “Answer these cards. Use Other once. Go back and change an answer.” Time task, note ambiguity and accidental taps. Ask whether every question earned its place.
4. “Explain the answer in your own words. How sure is it? Where did it come from?” Reveal Why only if they find it. Failure: believes this is guaranteed future knowledge or thinks headlines were verified full articles.
5. “Find what the app remembers. Correct one fact and delete another.” Reopen and check persistence. Ask if memory surprised them.
6. “Make a prediction card you'd send a friend. Preview it, but don't actually post.” Ask what should be excluded and which destination they would use. Sharing intention is not observed distribution.
7. Show randomized $5.99/$7.99 monthly test paywalls. “What would you get, what would it cost today, and how would you stop it?” Then “Would you choose free daily, subscription, packs or none? Why?” Stated intent is not willingness-to-pay proof.
8. “Find your old answer and tell the ball whether it happened.” Test check-back understanding without requesting permission before intent.

Acceptance: 4/5 complete ask/why/memory/share unaided; 5/5 understand forecasts can be wrong and test purchases charge nothing. Treat even one sensitive-topic scare or accidental real-looking charge as a stop-and-fix issue. Record completion, time, confusion, recalled price, response to uncertainty, privacy surprise and a verbatim short quote with permission. No demographic claims from n=5. Then run a larger opt-in beta to validate retention and costs before price experiments.

Time budgets (hypotheses): discover ask within 15s; 3-card flow <=90s and 10-card flow <=180s including thought time; find Why <=15s; edit/delete a fact <=45s; preview sharing <=30s; find History/check-back <=30s. A task counts unaided only with no moderator hint and within its budget. Price comprehension passes only if the participant correctly states the actual total charge, renewal interval, daily prediction cap and whether it is a subscription versus a one-off pack. Require 5/5 to identify test mode/no charge and >=4/5 to get every price field right. Ten cards may be burdensome; it is the maximum for context-heavy questions, not the default, and remains an untested mobile hypothesis.

Limit hypothesis: if at least 2 of 5 participants encounter the daily limit before creating or sharing a first satisfactory card, test a three-reading onboarding allowance before pricing is enabled. If at least 2 of 5 misunderstand the Plus cap or a pack as recurring, revise the offer copy before any real purchase flow. Neither limit is a measured audience norm.
