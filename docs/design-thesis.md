# Design thesis: the witch's house

Opening Crystal Ball means stepping into a witch's cottage at night. Every screen is a place in that house, lit by candles, with a glowing ball at its heart. This supersedes the Open mist direction and the Fraunces + Manrope pairing.

## Selected: B · Apothecary

The captain selected B on 2026-10-04. Use IM Fell English SC for headings and Caudex for body text. The room and ball are one scene: shelves behind, a table beneath the ball, room reflections in the glass, jars in front and a shared warm light. Tapping the ball pushes the room closer and crossfades to pre-blurred scenery; returning pulls back. History, Circles and plans are places in that room. Reduced motion uses a still composition. Device tilt remains a native verification gap; browser pointer and scroll parallax are separate evidence.

Parchment must have irregular edges, texture, thickness and shadow. Type remains real centered text. The question belongs on a physical slip by the stand, never flat on the sphere. The asset inventory and missing independent painted layers are recorded in `docs/art/apothecary-assets.md`.

## The rules

1. **Nothing is machine-cut.** No hairline rules, perfect rounded rectangles or straight dividers. Surfaces have hand-cut, chipped silhouettes. Edges are uneven, and dividers are carved grooves, twine or a row of stitches.
2. **Everything is made of something.** Each surface is a real material: aged wood planks, parchment, candle wax, brass, glass jars, dried herbs, iron nails. A flat colour block is never used as a surface.
3. **Candlelight is the only light.** Light is warm and comes from one source: the ball or a candle. It flickers slightly and falls off into shadow at the edges. There are no neutral greys and no cool UI blues.
4. **Few words, all of them lettered.** Each screen gets a headline of five words or fewer, plus at most one short line. Only omen and forecast explanations run long. Everything is centred.
5. **Game-like, not app-like.** Choices are big objects you pick up, like jars, cards, keys or charms. They use icons, pips and badges instead of labels and counters.

## Typography

There is no geometric or grotesk sans anywhere. Each concept uses a storybook or hand-lettered display face with a readable body face that has calligraphic character. All fonts are SIL OFL, with their licences saved next to them in `public/concepts/witch-assets/`. The IM Fell English Italic wordmark stays.

- Carved wood: Grenze Gotisch display, Alegreya body.
- Apothecary: IM Fell English SC labels, Caudex body.
- Storybook: Fondamento display, Gentium Book Plus body.

## Motion, sound, haptics

- Candle flicker: a slow, low-amplitude change in brightness. Dust motes drift through the light.
- Pressed objects wobble and settle, like a jar set down on a shelf, rather than scaling uniformly.
- Sound and haptics follow the [implemented feedback contract](design/motion.md#orb-delight); wood creaks, glass clinks and navigation page-turn sounds remain concept ideas.
- Reduce motion turns off flicker, dust and wobble, and keeps a static warm light.
- Target 60 fps. Textures are static images, and only opacity and transform animate.

## Honesty and accessibility

Prices, renewal and cancellation terms stay visible on a parchment tag. Icon-only objects keep accessibility labels. Text keeps 4.5:1 contrast against its actual texture, so lettering sits on darkened wood or light parchment and never on busy grain. Enlarging text to 200% reflows objects into a single centred column.

## Material roles within Apothecary

The working-cabinet study at `public/concepts/apothecary-materials.html` compares hanging labels, a cabinet and an open ledger. The implementation uses dark textured wood for primary actions, plain writing paper for fields, and the retained illustrated parchment for choices and information. History uses wide paper summaries on physical shelves, with the date and outcome on separate lines. Retained A/C paintings supply the adjacent shelf and hearth; an extracted B doorway moves as a foreground plane. This reuses existing art rather than claiming new painting or fully reconstructed room geometry.
