# Motion

The [motion language](motion-language.md) defines the shared entrance and feedback primitives, implementations, reference lessons, easing and timings.

`src/fogShader.ts` adapts the supplied reference into continuously advected domain-warped fog, without rotating the field. Outside the [room](#room), one persistent WebGL context draws the shared orbs, including escaping wisps. While visible with motion enabled, it runs at display cadence and keeps its clock across state changes. Glow, embers and sky discoveries animate only transforms/opacity. Native `FogTiles` moves three seamless translucent fields with the native driver beneath transparent glass artwork; native wisps recycle only at zero opacity. Run `node scripts/render-fog.mjs` to regenerate those assets. No frame-sequence assets or per-frame React updates are used.

Reduced motion freezes the fog, removes entrances and hides sky visitors. Either the system preference or in-app setting skips the approach delay. Large text caps shared decorative orbs at 96 pt; The room keeps its composition and its screens scroll the scaled text. The web fog stops scheduling animation callbacks while reduced motion is active, apart from one-off redraws for layout or content changes that apply the current mood immediately. Hiding the page cancels its pending callback; restoring visibility or disabling reduced motion resumes it without advancing through the paused interval. It cleans up on unmount. WebGL-unavailable browsers retain the still glass artwork. Native device frame-rate evidence is separate from compilation and browser proof.

## Room

Every tab is a spot in the approved AI-rendered library room (`src/RoomScene.web.tsx`, assets from `scripts/room-assets.mjs`): Home is the wide room with the ball on the desk, Sky the round skylight, Ask the ball close-up, History the scroll shelves, You the armchair and side table. Plus adds the Inner Library chamber behind the room, entered from Home by its push-in to the lectern's tome (no loop; leaving it cuts on web and cross-fades on native). A tab change plays the direct 1.5 s flight clip from the current spot (bundled, on web and native), then the destination's seamless 5 s loop where one is loaded; each flight starts and ends on its spots' stills, so still → flight → loop cuts without crossfades. On web a change during a flight, or before the clip has loaded, cuts straight to the destination still. Native holds the starting still while the clip loads; a failed load or a four-second timeout lands on the destination still. A new destination during a native flight cross-fades instead. Asset packaging, prefetch/cache behavior and hosting configuration: [Room renders](../../SETUP.md#room-renders).

The canvas Library glass ball is drawn live over the video in its own transparent canvas: each frame's tracked ball position, radius, view direction, depth-of-field blur and candle flicker (`src/roomTrack.json`) place and light it on the render's brass ring. Its procedural library room only lights and reflects in the glass: `scripts/bake-ball-env.mjs` renders it once as a half-float cube map (`assets/room/ball-env.bin`); rerun it whenever `scripts/ball-env-room.ts` changes. The ball itself (`src/glassBall.ts`) is shared by web (`src/glassWeb.ts`) and native (`src/glassNative.ts`, expo-gl). A Plus ball style replaces the procedural fog with that style's local-AI render of the ball (`assets/styles`): at rest the ball shows the render exactly; a spin turns the render's glint-free interior as an inner sphere while its rim and glints stay with the view, and the live reflection and candle highlight stay on top. Ball interaction and submission follow [user guidance](../../README.md); the typing camera and submission effects follow the [motion language](motion-language.md). Reduced motion plays no flights or loops, fades between stills (Web Animations, since the shell's reduced-motion rule stops CSS animations) and draws the ball one frame at a time; drag moves still redraw it. Hidden pages cancel pending callbacks. Without WebGL the static orb sits in the ring. Native plays the same flights with expo-video (muted, mixing with other audio) while the static orb rides each flight's track on the native driver (the iOS Simulator drops the GL ball over a playing video), then rests on the destination still with the live ball (no loops); navigation with reduced motion already enabled and destination changes mid-flight cross-fade the stills over 450 ms. Enabling reduced motion during a flight cuts directly to its destination on both platforms. A flight that never plays stops its player and lands after 4 s. Ask's listening waits until the native flight has landed and expo-video has released the audio session, so recording never starts under a playing clip. The ball is spun by touch, pauses in the background and has no depth-of-field blur. The tab bar's raised Ask orb is the same glass ball (`src/askOrbScene.ts`) inside a violet-gold aura, with stars and rim glints kept clear of its label; reduced motion draws it once, and its SVG ball stands in until GL draws.

## Orb delight

Gold editions add a rim/halo using the existing reveal entrance; exported cards and clips retain the speculative label. Static Plus skins tint the existing glass; they do not change the fog renderer.

Native orb touches use a soft impact; submitting a question and a newly completed reveal use a heavy impact; thinking pulses every half glow cycle. Ambient hum and reveal chime are optional, default off, pause during voice capture/backgrounding, and native playback respects the silent switch. Browser autoplay rejection is handled and a later deliberate tap can resume the enabled hum. Native haptic feel and silent-switch behavior still require device verification.
