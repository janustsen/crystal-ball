Store ratings are popularity signals, not proof of prediction accuracy. Prices can vary by offer and region. No purchases, accounts, or private data were used.

## Onboarding and retention proposal

Launch anonymously straight into Home. One line explains “A playful forecast, never a guarantee.” Tap the orb, enter a question, answer 3–10 relevant cards, watch real research status, reveal one short forecast. No birth-time form unless it is relevant (astrology is not our engine). Request notification permission only when the user sets a check-back. Offer Apple sign-in to preserve history after value is delivered. No contact upload.

The commercial wedge is accountable specificity: a date/number/pick, visible assumptions and source dates, and a later “Did it happen?” response. Avoid ominous mortality/illness statements and false claims of supernatural certainty. Sharing is opt-in and omits private answers. Explore begins with explicitly labeled editorial examples, never fake live user counts.

## Confidence and next evidence

High confidence: category features and listed prices above. Medium: reviews identify plausible friction, but highlighted reviews are neither representative nor necessarily recent. Low: conversion, retention and willingness to pay for this exact product. Before public release, conduct five 18–24 user sessions: unaided ask completion, why comprehension, meaning of confidence, memory-edit success and willingness to share. Success hypothesis: 4/5 complete unaided and understand forecasts can be wrong; no participant mistakes demo billing for a charge.

## Audience proxy, not product validation

[Pew's survey published May 21, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/) reports roughly one quarter of US adults aged 18–29 consult tarot annually; the overall adult figure for consulting astrology/tarot/fortune-tellers is 30%, generally for fun. Its representative fall-2024 survey supports an entertainment framing and a plausible young-adult audience, but 18–29 is not precisely 18–24 and it measures neither this app's demand nor subscription willingness. Sharing formats and $5.99 willingness-to-pay are still hypotheses. See user-test.md for explicit task/time/price-comprehension gates.

The exact featured Co–Star criticism is retained in evidence/costar-review-excerpt.txt (expanded accessibility snapshot; later screenshot had collapsed it). A systematic recent-review attempt via Apple's public US RSS endpoints returned no entries for all three comparables; evidence/review-sample.json records n=0. No complaint frequencies or ages are inferred. This failed source does not replace the explicitly pending target-user test. The Pew age18–29 proxy remains the only demographic evidence; it is not proof of18–24 willingness to pay.
