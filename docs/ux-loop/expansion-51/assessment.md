# Check-in hierarchy expansion while round50 is frozen

The study compares the current stacked outcome controls, a compact normal-text choice row that stacks at200%, and a saved-note view with an explicit Change outcome route. It uses only retained paper/wood art and the selected fonts. View it at /concepts/apothecary-check-in.html; screenshots cover all three compositions, draft/saved states and normal/200% text.

The saved note is the stronger candidate for already-recorded outcomes. It removes the simultaneous saved status, disabled Saved button and edit-instruction stack. At200%, the study content is1160px tall instead of1484px; this is a prototype composition measurement, not a production performance or usability claim. The exact forecast and uncertainty label remain above the saved outcome. Original question, check-in date, Why and Share remain available.

The compact choice row is a useful companion for normal text but should stack when text is enlarged. Its risk is shrinking radio targets or making selected state ambiguous. The stacked layout remains clearer where labels do not fit. A production implementation must measure targets, keep a redundant selected marker, preserve Arrow/Home/End/Space behavior and retain an explicit save step.

The saved-note route must support Change outcome, Cancel restoring the saved value, unsaved edits surviving only within the current editing session, explicit Save persistence, and focus/announcement on save/cancel. Prototype click handlers are not evidence for those production contracts. All current persistence, full-forecast, original-question and reminder assertions must survive; update drivers to use the visible Change outcome route rather than deleting their assertions.

The current illustration study keeps the paper readable and adds no new generated imagery. Production remains committed at round50 while its two blind reviews finish. No claim of9/10 follows from this study.
